Five art directions, from where we are to Rivet

The owner asked for directions that chase the Rivet concept's comic, hand-drawn quality, plus real backups that evolve the current riso print. This board compares five directions on one scene. For each direction it shows a Codex gameplay mock, a top-down Rivet at close-up and at 50 px, a Codex parts sheet run through our chroma key, and a frame rendered in the real Godot district with a prototype print shader.

The short answer. At gameplay scale (32 px/m, so Rivet is about 50 px across), none of the Rivet concept's hand-made detail survives on the character: hatching, grime and sketch lines all turn to mush. What survives is the silhouette, the vermilion hazard collar, value contrast, and a cream "sticker" rim. The comic look can live in the world (roofs, kerbs, vehicles, shadows) and in a calm print pass. Chase D4 Ink and Hatch in its hybrid form (hatched world, flat-wash streets, sticker-rimmed characters). Keep D1 Riso Refined as the safe backup. D2 Linocut is the bold alternative.

D1 Riso Refined closest evolution5/5

This is the current look with the mud taken out. The art is prompted as flat ink fills (the evolve round's own round-2 advice), and the full-screen pass uses a finer 3 px screen at 70% coverage with a flat vermilion plate, instead of dotting the accents.

Feasibility

What the shader must doKeep riso.gdshader, with a 3 px cell, ink density x0.7, and a flat vermilion plate (a threshold, not dots). It is done and proven in-engine as look=fine.
What the prompts must say"FLAT INK FILLS, bold even 3 px outlines, a fine light halftone used sparingly, plenty of bare paper." Add explicit bans: "teal only for ghosts", "vermilion E8502A, not orange".
Codex consistencyGood in structure, but the style drifts to clean vector and the palette leaks (orange, teal). The shader's palette snap repairs the palette.
Readability at 32 px/mGood on asphalt and weak on paper-coloured ground. It is the same as today, but lighter.
Migration effortAbout 1 hour. Swap the shader constants; the waves 1-4 assets stay. Optionally re-prompt future waves with "flat fills".
Can we get there5

D2 Linocut real backup: bold4/5

A carved block print: dark tones print as solid ink, light tones as paper, and gouges carry the form. It is the most graphic direction, it held the strictest palette of any direction across all four Codex images, and its bold outlines gave the best 50 px read.

Feasibility

What the shader must doThreshold the ink plate (at d > 0.40, solid), carve sparse lines into the solids with one cut direction per block, keep vermilion flat, and draw fat edges. It is prototyped as look=lino.
What the prompts must say"Hand-carved linocut, solid ink and carved white gouges, a flat off-register vermilion block, no halftone, no greys." For vehicles and props: "light bodies", because dark objects merge with black streets.
Codex consistencyThe best of the five. The palette held in 4 out of 4 images, the line weight matched across the mock, the sample, the parts and the sheet, and the sheet camera was perfect.
Readability at 32 px/mThe best character read. The risk is dark vehicles and props on black asphalt; they need a cream rim or light bodies.
Migration effortMedium. The shader is done. Regenerate the roofs (3 sheets) and the dark vehicles (about 3 sheets) for a threshold print, and recolour dark props. About a day.
Can we get there4

D3 Clear Line real backup: legibility4/5

Ligne claire: one even outline weight, flat fills and no texture. It is the most legible and the most machine-consistent direction, and the furthest from Rivet's grit. It needs about 7 inks, so it would break the owner's 4-ink lock; that is an owner call.

Feasibility

What the shader must doPre-blur, snap to the palette and outline. It is done as look=clear. The grain from the AI textures must be blurred out first.
What the prompts must say"Ligne claire, even outline weight, flat fills, no shading, no hatching, no halftone", plus a named palette.
Codex consistencyVery high. This is Codex's natural register.
Readability at 32 px/mHigh once the character values are fixed against the asphalt.
Migration effortLow for the shader; it works on today's assets. The identity cost is high: it drops the print and the grit.
Can we get there4

D4 Ink and Hatch chase this4/5

A pen-and-ink comic panel: hand-inked lines, hatching instead of dots, and two spot inks on cream paper. This is the Rivet concept's line and shading method, without the continuous-tone wash. The plain form hatches everything, which is busy at game scale. The hybrid form adds a tone budget: the streets are a flat ink wash, the hatching goes to roofs, kerbs and shadows, and the characters are flat-value cut-outs with a cream sticker rim.

Feasibility

What the shader must doTwo jobs. (1) Set the tone budget: map ink density to 3 flat wash steps and hatch only in deep shadow (look=wash, done). (2) Keep the item pass from dotting the characters: snap steel to a flat value, not a 2 px screen (look_item mode 5, done). The pen work itself must come from the art. When the shader adds hatching (look=hatch) it is world-locked through a world_px uniform, so it does not swim, but it hatches the asphalt too.
What the prompts must sayThe D4 style block, plus "hatching is a budget: the asphalt is a flat mid-grey ink wash, with the densest pen work on the roofs". For characters: "seen from directly above, only the top of each helmet; NO faces, visors or chibi figures; a flat light value with a thick cream sticker rim and an ink outline". For props: "rust is ink, not vermilion".
Codex consistencyGood. Six D4 images (two mocks, the hybrid, the sample, the parts and the sheet) kept the same line, hatch and paper. The first hybrid run was rejected because the characters came back as three-quarter chibis with visors; the explicit character-camera rule fixed it on the next run. Vermilion overuse is the main drift.
Readability at 32 px/mThe plain form is poor (busy hatching and lost hatch detail on characters). The hybrid is the best on the board.
Migration effortAbout 2 days. The shader is done. Regenerate the roofs (3 sheets), vehicles (3), props (3) and ground decals (2) at about 1.5 minutes per sheet. Regenerate the Rivet and King parts sheets in D4 with the sticker rim. Re-key and re-slice with the existing tools.
Can we get there4

D5 Inked Grime full chase2/5

The Rivet concept as a game: sketchy ink, watercolour-like wash, grime, rust and misregistered vermilion. Codex can draw it, and it stays in style across images. The problem is the scale: almost everything that makes the concept beautiful is smaller than one screen pixel at 32 px/m.

Feasibility

What the shader must doVery little that helps. It can quantise a wash and add grit (look=wash), but it cannot create sketch lines or grime that the art does not carry, and a print pass would destroy the continuous tone that defines the look.
What the prompts must sayAttach the concept as a style reference every time. Add "cream paper ground, no coloured light, no sodium glow" and "ink for rust, not vermilion".
Codex consistencyThe style is good across 3 images; the palette drifts in every one.
Readability at 32 px/mPoor. The detail is sub-pixel and the value contrast with the ground is low.
Migration effortHigh. Every asset must be regenerated, the print identity is lost, and a new readability system must be designed.
Can we get there2

Where D5 does belong: key art, the loading screens, the soul-selector portraits and HUD cards. These are 192 px and larger, where its craft is visible.

What the 50 px test says, across all five

  • Hatching, grime and sketch lines do not survive 50 px. D4's hatched Rivet and D5's grimy Rivet both collapse to a value blob with a collar. Put the comic craft into the world, where surfaces are large (roofs at 80 px/m, vehicles at 48 px/m), not into the characters.
  • Bold outlines survive. D2's fat carved outline is the only internal detail that holds at 50 px.
  • The sticker rim is the readability system, and it is style-independent. Keep it in every direction: add it at keying time (a 2 px cream dilation plus a 1 px ink line) or in the item shader.
  • The ground decides. A cream character dies on cream paper; a dark one dies on asphalt. Every direction needs a tone budget in which the street sits one clear value step away from the characters.

Recommendation

Chase first: D4 Ink and Hatch, hybrid form. It is the closest we can get to the Rivet concept that still plays at 32 px/m. It uses the concept's line and its hatching where they are visible (roofs, kerbs, vehicles, shadows), a calm flat-wash street, and flat-value, sticker-rimmed characters. Codex holds the style across sheets, parts and mocks; the print pass is already prototyped in-engine; and the cutout pipeline keys the parts cleanly (4/4 parts, about 1.5% spill).

Safe backup: D1 Riso Refined. It is one shader change (proven as look=fine), with zero asset migration, and the current waves stay. It does not chase Rivet, but it fixes today's muddiness.

Bold alternative if D4 stalls: D2 Linocut. It has the best Codex palette discipline and the best 50 px read, and it is hand-made in a different way.

Method, budget and honesty notes

  • Images: 20 Codex image_gen calls, run one at a time; 19 used and 1 rejected (d4h_mock: character camera). There were no usage or rate-limit events. Each call took 70-180 s (mean about 100 s) and used about 100-175k input tokens, mostly cached. Every image was inspected before use.
  • Item-level camera drift that was accepted, not rejected:
    • water-tank legs read slightly oblique in most of the 7 accepted mocks (accepted as GTA 2 convention, as in the evolve round);
    • lamp posts lean in the two plain D4 mocks;
    • D2's ghosts are face-on and its cops show their visors;
    • D4's torso part has a small front bevel.
    The 2 environment sheets, whose prompt says "lying flat so that only its TOP surface shows", came back 8/8 top-down, lamps and tanks included.
  • References:
    • Every mock attached ref-1-camera-90-TARGET.jpg as Image 1.
    • The Rivet design came from the v2 turnaround (D1-D4) or the concept (D5).
    • Each parts sheet attached that direction's own Rivet sample as the style lock.
    The prompts to regenerate every image are in _prompts/<direction>.md.
  • The 50 px panels crop the character from its paper with a border-connected flood fill and resize the long side to 50 px (Lanczos). They are shown at 1x on paper and on asphalt #585258, then 4x nearest.
  • The composites are offline (PIL), not in-engine: the sample is pasted at 50 px into the in-engine frame of the matching print pass. The real rig would also pass through the item shader.
  • The in-engine frames are the deterministic --autoplay --district hook (junction t=16 s, roofs t=9.5 s), rendered on d3d12 from the artdir worktree with --look=riso|fine|hatch|wash|lino|clear. The shaders are game/look.gdshader and game/look_item.gdshader; main.gd has a 21-line diff and the default path is untouched. They are committed on branch artdir (commits 2215081, 25b9a97, 821a3e7). The renders are reproducible with gtai-spike/artdir/tools/render.sh <look>.
  • Key metrics come from evolve/tools/proc.py. Codex's "#00B140" came back as anything from (1,167,65) to (1,204,35), but it was always flat (border std about 1), so the border-measured key worked on all 7 sheets: 4/4 parts in each of the 5 parts sheets and 8/8 objects in each of the 2 environment sheets.
